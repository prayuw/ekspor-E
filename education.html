Memahami HS Code
Trade Basics · 2h 30m · Guidance article & quick quiz

What is an HS Code?

The Harmonized System (HS) is an internationally standardized system of names and numbers used to classify traded products. Developed by the World Customs Organization (WCO), it is used by more than 200 countries to classify over 98% of global merchandise trade.

Structure of an HS Code (6 digits)

0901.21
09 = Chapter (Coffee, tea, maté and spices)
0901 = Heading (Coffee, whether or not roasted or decaffeinated)
0901.21 = Subheading (Coffee, roasted, not decaffeinated)

Example: Arabica Coffee Export

For specialty Arabica Coffee exported from Indonesia to Japan, the correct HS Code is 0901.21. Some countries add 2 or 4 more digits for tariff statistical purposes (e.g. 0901.21.00.00 in Japan).

Tips: Always verify the HS Code with your destination country's customs tariff schedule — small classification differences can change tariffs by 5–15%.

SPS Requirements for Food Products
Compliance · 1h 45m · Guidance article & quick quiz

What is SPS?

Sanitary and Phytosanitary (SPS) measures are the laws, regulations, standards and procedures that governments use to protect human, animal and plant life from pests, diseases and food safety risks in international trade.

The WTO SPS Agreement

The WTO Agreement on the Application of Sanitary and Phytosanitary Measures (1995) sets out the basic rules for food safety and animal/plant health standards. It allows countries to set their own SPS standards, but they must be science-based, applied only to the extent necessary, and not arbitrarily discriminatory.

Example: Arabica Coffee to Japan

Japan SPS requirements for green/roasted coffee:
  • Phytosanitary Certificate (issued by Indonesian quarantine authority)
  • Maximum residue limits (MRLs) on pesticides — checked against Japan's Positive List
  • Aflatoxin testing (for some categories) — max 10 ppb
  • Food Sanitation Act compliance labelling in Japanese
  • Manufacturing/HACCP-equivalent process documentation
Pre-export checklist: Phytosanitary cert · MRL lab test · Aflatoxin test · Japan-language label · HACCP docs.

Mastering Incoterms 2020
Logistics · 2h 00m · Guidance article

What are Incoterms?

Incoterms (International Commercial Terms) are internationally recognized rules published by the International Chamber of Commerce (ICC) that define the responsibilities of buyers and sellers in international trade. The current version, Incoterms 2020, lists 11 terms.

The 11 Incoterms 2020

Rules for any mode of transport:
  • EXW — Ex Works
  • FCA — Free Carrier
  • CPT — Carriage Paid To
  • CIP — Carriage and Insurance Paid To
  • DAP — Delivered at Place
  • DPU — Delivered at Place Unloaded
  • DDP — Delivered Duty Paid
Rules for sea and inland waterway transport:
  • FAS — Free Alongside Ship
  • FOB — Free on Board
  • CFR — Cost and Freight
  • CIF — Cost, Insurance and Freight

Example: Selling Arabica Coffee to Japan

For a coffee MSME exporting FCL containers to Japan, FOB (Free on Board) or CIF (Cost, Insurance and Freight) are most common. Under FOB, the seller's responsibility ends once goods are loaded onto the vessel at the Indonesian port. Under CIF, the seller also pays for freight and minimum insurance to the destination port.

Tips: Always agree the Incoterm in writing on your Proforma Invoice and Sales Contract — never leave it implied.
Export Documentation Checklist
Documentation · 1h 30m · Guidance article

Why documentation matters

Accurate documentation is the backbone of any export shipment. Missing or incorrect paperwork causes delays at customs, demurrage charges, and in some cases seizure of goods. For Indonesian agro exports, the following documents are typically required.

Core commercial documents

  • Proforma Invoice — preliminary quote sent to the buyer before the order is confirmed.
  • Commercial Invoice — the official bill for the goods, used by customs to assess duties.
  • Packing List — itemizes the contents, weight, and dimensions of each package.
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB) — transport document and title to the goods.

Export-specific & regulatory documents

  • Certificate of Origin (COO) — often issued by the Indonesian Chamber of Commerce (KADIN); required for preferential tariffs under trade agreements.
  • Phytosanitary Certificate — issued by the Indonesian quarantine authority (Barantan) for plant-based products.
  • Health Certificate — for processed food products.
  • Form E / Form D / Form A — preferential origin certificates for ASEAN, ASEAN–China, or GSP markets.
  • PEB (Pemberitahuan Ekspor Barang) — the Indonesian customs export declaration filed through CEISA.
Checklist: Proforma Invoice · Commercial Invoice · Packing List · B/L · COO · Phytosanitary Cert · Form D/E · PEB.
Digital Catalog 101
Digital Readiness · 45 min · Guidance article

Why a digital catalog?

International buyers almost always research suppliers online before contacting them. A professional digital catalog — with high-quality photos, specifications, HS codes, and certifications — is often the first impression a buyer has of your business.

What to include

  • Product photos (minimum 3 angles, white background)
  • Product name, HS Code, origin, grade
  • Specs: packaging, weight, shelf life
  • Certifications held (Halal, HACCP, Organic)
  • Minimum order quantity (MOQ) and lead time
  • Incoterms and payment terms
CBAM & ESG Reporting
Sustainability · 1h 20m · Guidance article

What is CBAM?

The Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) is a EU policy that places a carbon price on imported goods from outside the EU. While agro products are not yet in scope, sustainability reporting and traceability are increasingly demanded by EU buyers as part of ESG due diligence.

What MSMEs should prepare

  • Farm-level traceability data (origin, harvest date, plot)
  • Energy and water use documentation per kg of product
  • Third-party sustainability certification (Rainforest Alliance, Organic)
  • Annual ESG disclosure template
